REFERENCES USED FOR FEAR GUIDE

Primary official references
1. NHS. Get help with anxiety, fear or panic.
   https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/feelings-symptoms-behaviours/feelings-and-symptoms/anxiety-fear-panic/
   Why used: provides official guidance that fear can involve stress hormones and physical symptoms, and that people should not self-diagnose. It supports calm coping and help-seeking when fear is difficult to manage.

2. NHS. Breathing exercises for stress.
   https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/
   Why used: supports slow, gentle breathing as a practical calming strategy that can be done in different positions and settings.

3. NHS inform. Grounding exercises.
   https://www.nhsinform.scot/healthy-living/mental-wellbeing/breathing-and-relaxation-exercises/grounding-exercises/
   Why used: supports grounding techniques, including sensory grounding, for anxiety and stress when feelings become overwhelming.

4. WHO. Doing What Matters in Times of Stress: An Illustrated Guide. 2020.
   https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789240003927
   Why used: supports grounding, unhooking from difficult thoughts and feelings, acting on values, and making room for difficult experiences.

5. CDC. Managing Difficult Emotions.
   https://www.cdc.gov/emotional-well-being/managing-difficult-emotions/index.html
   Why used: includes fear as a difficult emotion and supports identifying and challenging fears using cognitive-behavioral approaches.

6. CDC. Improve Your Emotional Well-Being.
   https://www.cdc.gov/emotional-well-being/improve-your-emotional-well-being/index.html
   Why used: supports managing difficult emotions, healthy stress coping, relaxation techniques, journaling, and asking for support.

Supporting background
7. American Psychological Association. Anxiety.
   https://www.apa.org/topics/anxiety
   Why used: explains anxiety as involving tension, worried thoughts, and physical changes. This supports body-first calming and present-moment grounding for fear-related states.

8. Gross JJ. The emerging field of emotion regulation: an integrative review. Psychological Review. 1998;105(2):271-299.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.105.2.271
   Why used: provides the emotion regulation basis for attention deployment, cognitive change, and response modulation.

9. Beck AT. Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders. 1979.
   Why used: provides the CBT basis for identifying and reducing unhelpful fear-related thoughts without validating catastrophic interpretations.

10. Hofmann SG, Asnaani A, Vonk IJJ, Sawyer AT, Fang A. The efficacy of cognitive behavioral therapy: a review of meta-analyses. World Psychiatry. 2012;11(3):427-440.
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.20002
   Why used: supports CBT as an evidence-based framework for emotional difficulties, including anxiety-related fear.

NOTE
The fear guide is designed for short wellbeing nudges, not therapy, diagnosis, exposure treatment, or crisis response. It uses conservative actions such as grounding, slow breathing, calming the body, present-moment orientation, and trusted support.
